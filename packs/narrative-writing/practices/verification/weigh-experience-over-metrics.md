## When to apply

Apply whenever a pass-or-fail judgment about narrative quality is being made: evaluating a scene,
accepting a draft, or deciding whether a metric suite's verdict settles the question. Apply with
extra care when every tracked metric is green and the item is about to be closed.

## Guidance

- Ask the experience question first: does the intended audience want to keep going, and do they stay
  for the characters rather than for the twist? Check the metrics second.
- Treat every metric as necessary but not sufficient. A green audit is not evidence that the drama
  works. When metrics pass and the experience fails, the experience wins and the item is not done.
- When something underperforms, fix function before volume: give the beat a job, a turn, or a cost
  instead of padding word count to clear a length target.
- Do not add new quantitative metrics unless the owner explicitly asks. Newly noticed problems go
  into containers that already exist.
- Keep a metric-boundary clause in the criteria document: metrics bound the search; they do not
  define quality.

## Why

Long-form fiction usually dies by being correct rather than compelling. A metric suite measures the
absence of known defects; it cannot measure attachment. A draft can clear every rule - no priority
issues, budgets met, topic audit green - and still give the audience no reason to care what happens
next. Reading the numbers as the goal optimizes the draft for the numbers.

## Exceptions and boundaries

Experience-first is not a license to skip the metrics: they catch defects cheaply (broken continuity,
missing setups, budget overruns) and their failures are real. Nor is it a license for unlimited
polish - the frozen exit criteria still bound the work. Use experience judgment to rule on items the
metrics passed, not to reopen items the metrics legitimately failed.

## Example

A draft passed the full rule set and a playtest note still said the reader had no reason to stay past
the first hour. The fix was not more words: the scene was rebuilt so the beat turned on the
character's choice and its cost, and the audience had a reason to follow her rather than the mystery.
The rule that came out of it: experience question first, metrics second, and all-green recorded as
not sufficient.
